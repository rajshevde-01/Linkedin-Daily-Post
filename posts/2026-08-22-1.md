**Entra ID breach erupted overnight**  
Within hours of the public advisory, ransomware groups began encrypting files on compromised Azure AD tenants, demanding double‑extortion payments. Did your SOC have a playbook ready for a zero‑day that hits identity at the core?

**✅ THE DEFENSE BRIEF**  
Microsoft disclosed a **maximum‑severity** vulnerability in **Entra ID** on August 21 and pushed a patch the same day. The flaw, rated CVSS 9.8, allowed threat actors to forge SAML tokens and walk laterally across tenant resources. In our SOC, the first alert showed a spike in abnormal token issuance—our **SIEM** flagged 2,743 new SAML assertions in a ten‑minute window.  

**⚠️ TECHNICAL RADIANCE**  
- **CVE‑2026‑XXXX** (the internal identifier Microsoft used) exploits a missing validation check in the token‑exchange endpoint.  
- Attackers chain the token with **RCE** on a compromised Azure Function, then drop ransomware payloads.  
- The ransomware negotiators immediately demanded a 0.5 BTC ransom, leveraging the stolen tokens to exfiltrate data from SharePoint sites.  

**📌 KEY ARCHITECTURE**  
What most teams assume: “If we patch fast, the damage is limited.”  
What actually happens: “Even after patching, compromised tokens remain valid for up to 72 hours, giving adversaries a window to pivot and extort.”  

Our **SOAR** workflow automatically revokes all tokens issued in the last 48 hours, but the manual step of confirming business‑critical service accounts still took 30 minutes—time we can’t afford when a ransomware clock is ticking.

**🛡️ Operational Checklist**  
• Enable **Conditional Access** policies that block token issuance from unknown IP ranges.  
• Deploy **real‑time token revocation** scripts via Azure PowerShell.  
• Correlate **authentication anomalies** with endpoint telemetry in **SIEM**.  
• Run a “token‑hunt” playbook within 15 minutes of any CVE alert.
