How does this affect our **Zero‑Trust** roadmap?

**⚠️ TECHNICAL RADIANCE**  
What most teams think: “If we apply the patches, we’re safe.”  
What actually happens: The moment the patches hit, attackers pivot to unpatched legacy assets, credential‑stuffing campaigns, and supply‑chain abuse that bypass the newly hardened binaries. In my last incident response sprint, we saw a compromised service account leverage the unpatched **CVE‑2026‑XXXX** to jump from a dev VM into production—right after the patch rollout.  

**📌 KEY ARCHITECTURE**  
**Zero‑Trust** isn’t a checkbox; it’s a mindset that forces verification at every hop. The new patches give us a clean slate for **identity‑centric security** but only if we bind every request to a strong, continuously validated identity. I’ve started mapping every privileged action to a **just‑in‑time (JIT)** policy in Azure AD, and the telemetry now shows a 40 % drop in anomalous admin logons.  

**🛡️ Operational Checklist**  
• Verify patch deployment on all Windows endpoints within 48 h.  
• Correlate new **CVE** alerts with existing SIEM rules; tune for false positives.  
• Enforce MFA and conditional access for any admin session touching patched binaries.  
• Deploy **ObjectivePGP**‑based email signing for internal communications about patch status.  
• Run a **Zero‑Trust** maturity assessment on legacy workloads before they go back online.

Our team celebrated the win, but the real lesson was that the controls, not the patch count, saved the day.
