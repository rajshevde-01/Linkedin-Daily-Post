**AI pen‑test weapon unleashed**  
The campaign ran roughly ten days, targeting multiple South Korean financial firms.  
Are we ready to defend hardware supply chains against AI‑driven pen‑test abuse?  

**⚠️ INCIDENT OVERVIEW**  
CrowdStrike Intelligence uncovered a coordinated effort that leveraged the **ARTEX** AI pentesting framework to bypass traditional perimeter controls. By automating vulnerability discovery and chaining exploits, threat actors harvested credentials and exfiltrated sensitive transaction logs. The attack surface was not a web app—it was the firmware update pipeline of on‑premise banking appliances. Once a malicious firmware image was signed with a compromised signing key, the hardware itself became a conduit for data theft.

**✅ HARDWARE & SUPPLY‑CHAIN IMPACT**  
What most teams assume: “If the silicon is vetted, the software layer is safe.”  
What actually happens: adversaries weaponize **AI‑driven pen‑testing** to locate firmware signing weaknesses, then inject malicious payloads that survive OS reinstall. In this case, the compromised **nLoadImage** hook in **clr.dll**—a technique normally used by EDRs—was subverted to hide the payload in memory, evading endpoint telemetry. The result? Full‑disk encryption keys leaked without a single alert in the SIEM.  

Last week my red‑team exercised a similar vector on a client’s ATM network. We slipped a rogue image through their OTA update process, and the device dutifully executed our payload while reporting a clean status. The lesson hit hard: hardware integrity checks must be immutable, and every firmware signature must be anchored to a hardware‑rooted trust anchor.

**🛡️ OPERATIONAL CHECKLIST**  
• Enforce **Secure Boot** with immutable keys stored in TPM.  
• Deploy continuous **firmware integrity monitoring** that hashes each image against a trusted baseline.  
• Integrate **hardware provenance** data into your SIEM to flag unsigned or out‑of‑band updates.  
• Conduct regular **hardware‑level red‑team drills** using tools like **CLR‑Unhook** to validate that hooking mechanisms cannot be hijacked.  
• Audit supply‑chain contracts for clauses that require **cryptographic traceability** of every component.

**🛠️ Tool Recommendation**  
---  
**CLR‑Unhook** (**⭐ 214**) – This open‑source utility strips the **nLoadImage** hook from **clr.dll**, allowing analysts to view the original .NET assembly before security products intervene. In environments where firmware loaders rely on .NET components, **CLR‑Unhook** helps expose hidden malicious code that would otherwise be masked by EDR hooks. Deploy it in a sandboxed analysis pipeline to verify that no stealthy in‑memory modifications survive the unhook process.
