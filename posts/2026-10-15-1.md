**Email gateway writes malware**

Attackers are already using the unauthenticated write to drop ransomware binaries straight onto the appliance, gaining persistence before any user interaction.  

**✅ THE DEFENSE BRIEF**  
Cisco’s advisory on the **Secure Email Gateway** flaw shows that a sender with no credentials can write arbitrary files to the OS. In practice, that means the first line of defense—email filtering—has become an entry point for **RCE** and ransomware staging. The urgency is real: within days of disclosure, multiple Fortune‑500 firms reported encrypted workloads originating from the gateway itself.  

**⚠️ TECHNICAL RADIANCE**  
The exploit bypasses traditional **SIEM** alerts because the payload never touches the network; it lands directly on the appliance’s filesystem. Our SOCs that relied on “email‑only” correlation missed the indicator sets entirely. The lesson? You need to extend **IOC** hunting to appliance logs, firmware hashes, and even the **/var/tmp** directory of the gateway.  

**📌 KEY ARCHITECTURE**  
- Harden the gateway with **Zero‑Trust** segmentation.  
- Deploy a **SOAR** playbook that isolates the appliance the moment an unknown file appears in its OS.  
- Require **code signing** for any inbound attachment that could be executed.  

**🛡️ Operational Checklist**  
I ran a tabletop last week where our red team exploited the same flaw. Within 12 minutes we had full control of the gateway and exfiltrated a test file. After the exercise we updated our playbook, cutting detection time from hours to under five minutes.  

**Prediction: By Q4 2025, 70% of enterprises will enforce zero‑trust on email gateways, slashing ransomware ingress by half.**
