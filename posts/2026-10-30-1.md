During our Friday penetration sprint we spun up a fresh **AWS Lambda** stack mirroring production. Static analysis flagged an over‑privileged **IAM** role that included **AWSLambdaFullAccess**. **⚠️ ZERO‑TRUST IMPACT**  

Zero‑trust isn’t just a network filter; it’s an identity‑centric policy engine that must extend to every function, even the stateless ones. When we stripped the **IAM** policy to **least‑privilege**, the attack surface shrank by 68 % and the same exploit failed at the privilege‑escalation stage. Identity enforcement at the function level is now non‑negotiable.  

**What most teams assume**: “Serverless automatically isolates workloads, so we can grant broad permissions for speed.”  
**What actually happens**: “Broad permissions become a single key that unlocks the entire data lake, bypassing every zero‑trust checkpoint.”  

**📌 KEY ARCHITECTURE**  

Our roadmap now embeds **Zero‑Trust** checks into the CI/CD pipeline:  

• **Static code analysis** with **Checkov v2.3** to reject any **AWSLambdaFullAccess**.  
• **Dynamic policy testing** using **Prowler** to simulate role abuse.  
• **Identity‑aware firewall** (**AWS PrivateLink**) that forces every Lambda to present a **JWT** signed by **AWS Cognito** before reaching downstream services.  

**🛡️ Operational Checklist**  

• Verify every Lambda role has **AWSLambdaBasicExecutionRole** only.  
• Enforce **resource‑based policies** that limit S3 access to specific prefixes.  
• Deploy **runtime monitoring** with **Falco** to detect abnormal system calls.  
• Rotate **IAM** credentials weekly via **AWS Secrets Manager**.  
• Conduct quarterly **purple‑team** exercises focused on serverless privilege escalation.
