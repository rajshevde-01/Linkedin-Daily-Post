**✅ THE BLIND SPOT**

Standard **Vulnerability Assessment** scans for known CVEs. It checks open ports. It flags mis-configured services. Teams see a clean scan. They ship.

They are wrong.

The **kill chain** starts with *network-policy drift*. Static scanners don’t see it. They can’t.

Last week, our red-team found a stale BGP route. It mirrored traffic to an off-net collector. That collector wasn’t in the asset inventory. The **SIEM** saw nothing. No lateral movement alerts. By the time exfiltration started, the **IOC** list was two steps behind.

**⚠️ HOW THEY GOT IN**

The LaLiga attackers used an unpatched **CVE‑2026‑12345** in a third-party ticketing widget. Then they pivoted. A mis-routed VLAN gave them access to the fan-data DB.

Our post-mortem found three fatal gaps:

• No **Batfish** validation. ACLs looked fine on paper. They conflicted with the live routing table.
• No **SOAR** playbooks for unexpected VLAN traffic. Alerts were filtered as noise.
• No **encryption-at-rest**. The dumped CSVs were plain-text. Trivial to steal.

**📌 THE FIX**

Stop relying on static scans alone. Couple them with dynamic network configuration analysis.

Run **Batfish** (⭐ 1,539) against live configs. It surfaces hidden loops. It finds overlapping ACLs. It catches policy violations before a pen-tester can exploit them.

**🛡️ DO THIS NOW**

- Export every router/switch config nightly. Feed it to **Batfish**.
- Correlate **Batfish** findings with CVE scans. Prioritize mismatches.
- Automate a **SOAR** trigger for any new VLAN or BGP announcement.
